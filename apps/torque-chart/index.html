Highlighted row matches the bolt above. Tap any size to look it up. Dry clean threads, plain or zinc-plated, as they come. Oiled threads with engine oil on them (25% less). How it's worked out: torque = K × diameter × clamp load, where the clamp load is 75% of the bolt's proof load (SAE J429, ISO 898-1) and K is 0.20 dry or 0.15 oiled. Charts that use other assumptions will differ a little. Don't use these numbers for:
  • head bolts, connecting rods, flywheels, wheels and anything with a spec in the manual
  • torque-to-yield bolts (torque plus an angle), which are often single use
  • stainless steel, brass or aluminum bolts
  • bolts threaded into aluminum, cast iron or plastic, or through gaskets
  • anti-seize (it lowers friction even more than oil; follow its maker's advice)
